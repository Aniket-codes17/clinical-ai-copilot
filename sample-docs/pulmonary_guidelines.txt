Pulmonary Conditions — Chest X-Ray Findings & Management Guidelines

CONSOLIDATION
Definition: Replacement of alveolar air with fluid, pus, blood, or cells — appears as homogeneous opacification with air bronchograms.
Common causes: Bacterial pneumonia, aspiration, pulmonary infarction, lung contusion, alveolar haemorrhage.
Chest X-Ray Features:
- Homogeneous opacification, lobar or segmental distribution.
- Air bronchograms (pathognomonic of alveolar filling).
- Loss of silhouette sign (right middle lobe: right heart border; lingula: left heart border).
Management:
- Treat underlying cause (antibiotics for pneumonia, anticoagulation for infarct).
- Repeat CXR at 6-8 weeks to confirm resolution and exclude underlying malignancy.

INTERSTITIAL INFILTRATES / DIFFUSE BILATERAL INFILTRATES
Causes: Viral pneumonia (COVID-19, influenza), atypical pneumonia (Mycoplasma, Legionella), pulmonary fibrosis, ARDS, heart failure.
Management of ARDS (Berlin criteria: PaO2/FiO2 <300 + bilateral infiltrates + not explained by cardiac failure):
- Low tidal volume ventilation: 6 mL/kg IBW, plateau pressure <30 cmH2O.
- PEEP: titrate to oxygenation using ARDSnet PEEP/FiO2 tables.
- Prone positioning: ≥16h/day if PaO2/FiO2 <150 on FiO2 ≥0.6 (reduces mortality by 16%).
- Neuromuscular blockade: Cisatracurium infusion in first 48h if PaO2/FiO2 <150.
- Dexamethasone 6 mg OD for 10 days in moderate-severe ARDS (DEXA-ARDS trial).
- Avoid: fluid overload, high driving pressures, high FiO2 (target SpO2 88-95%).

PNEUMOTHORAX
Types: Primary spontaneous (young, tall, thin males), secondary (COPD, asthma, Marfan's), tension, traumatic.
CXR features: Visible lung edge without vascular markings, deep sulcus sign (supine).
Management:
- Small (<2 cm, stable): observe, 100% O2 accelerates resorption, follow-up CXR at 2-4h.
- Large or symptomatic: aspiration (14G cannula, 2nd intercostal space, mid-clavicular line) or chest drain.
- Tension pneumothorax: immediate needle decompression (do not wait for CXR), then chest drain.
- Recurrent: referral for VATS pleurodesis/pleurectomy.

LUNG NODULE / MASS
Fleischner Society Guidelines for Incidental Pulmonary Nodules (2017):
- <6 mm, low risk: no routine follow-up.
- 6-8 mm, low risk: CT at 6-12 months, then 18-24 months.
- >8 mm, any risk: CT at 3 months; consider PET-CT and/or biopsy.
- High-risk features: spiculated margins, upper lobe location, history of smoking/malignancy.
- Solid nodule vs subsolid (ground-glass): ground-glass has higher likelihood of adenocarcinoma spectrum; shorter follow-up intervals.
Biopsy indications: Nodule >1 cm, PET avid, growing on serial imaging, high pre-test probability of malignancy.

CHRONIC OBSTRUCTIVE PULMONARY DISEASE (COPD) EXACERBATION
Definition: Acute worsening of respiratory symptoms beyond normal day-to-day variation requiring change in medication.
CXR: Hyperinflation (>6 ribs anteriorly, flattened diaphragm), increased anteroposterior diameter, bullae. Exclude pneumonia, pneumothorax, pulmonary oedema.
Management:
- Bronchodilators: SABA (Salbutamol 2.5mg NEB q4-6h) + SAMA (Ipratropium 500mcg NEB TID-QID).
- Systemic corticosteroids: Prednisolone 40 mg OD x5 days (reduces treatment failure, improves FEV1).
- Antibiotics: if purulent sputum, increased breathlessness, or increased sputum volume (Amoxicillin, Doxycycline, or Azithromycin 5 days).
- Controlled oxygen: target SpO2 88-92% (avoid hyperoxia — suppresses hypoxic drive).
- NIV (BiPAP): if pH <7.35 + PaCO2 >6 kPa despite initial therapy (reduces intubation and mortality).
- DVT prophylaxis: LMWH (Enoxaparin 40 mg SC OD).
- Discharge planning: pulmonary rehab referral, smoking cessation, inhaler technique review, influenza and pneumococcal vaccine.

PULMONARY EMBOLISM
Wells Score: (DVT symptoms +3, HR >100 +1.5, immobilisation/surgery last 4 weeks +1.5, previous PE/DVT +1.5, haemoptysis +1, malignancy +1, alternative diagnosis less likely +3).
- Score >4: High probability. Score ≤4: Low-moderate probability.
Investigations:
- D-dimer: sensitive but not specific; negative D-dimer (with low pre-test probability) excludes PE.
- CTPA: investigation of choice for confirming PE.
- V/Q scan: if CTPA contraindicated (contrast allergy, renal failure, pregnancy).
- Echo: right heart strain (RV dilation, McConnell's sign, D-shaped septum) in massive/submassive PE.
Management:
- Haemodynamically stable: DOAC (Rivaroxaban 15 mg BD x21 days then 20 mg OD, or Apixaban 10 mg BD x7 days then 5 mg BD).
- LMWH bridge + warfarin: if DOAC not suitable (antiphospholipid syndrome, renal failure, pregnancy — use LMWH throughout).
- Massive PE (haemodynamic instability): systemic thrombolysis (Alteplase 100 mg IV over 2h) if no contraindications. Surgical embolectomy or catheter-directed thrombolysis if thrombolysis failed/contraindicated.
- Duration: provoked PE — 3 months. Unprovoked — 6-12 months minimum; consider indefinite anticoagulation with DOAC if low bleeding risk.

TUBERCULOSIS (TB)
CXR features (Post-primary): Upper lobe consolidation/cavitation, fibrosis, calcified nodes, miliary pattern (1-2mm nodules throughout).
Diagnosis: Sputum AFB smear and culture (gold standard), GeneXpert MTB/RIF (rapid, detects rifampicin resistance), Mantoux test, IGRA (QuantiFERON-TB Gold).
Treatment (Drug-sensitive TB):
- Initiation phase (2 months): HRZE (Isoniazid + Rifampicin + Pyrazinamide + Ethambutol).
- Continuation phase (4 months): HR (Isoniazid + Rifampicin).
- Pyridoxine 25 mg OD with Isoniazid to prevent peripheral neuropathy.
- Monthly sputum smear/culture to monitor response.
- Notify public health authority. Contact tracing mandatory.
Drug-resistant TB: MDR-TB (resistant to H+R) — bedaquiline-containing regimens under specialist supervision.

Community-Acquired Pneumonia (CAP) — Clinical Guidelines (ATS/IDSA 2019)

DEFINITION
Community-acquired pneumonia is an acute infection of the pulmonary parenchyma acquired outside a hospital or healthcare facility.

DIAGNOSIS
- Chest radiograph: gold standard; shows lobar consolidation, interstitial infiltrates, or multilobar opacities.
- CT chest: more sensitive; use when X-ray is non-diagnostic and clinical suspicion is high.
- Sputum Gram stain and culture: obtain before antibiotics in ICU patients.
- Blood cultures: recommended for ICU admission, cavitary infiltrates, leukopenia, or active alcohol abuse.
- Urinary antigen tests: Legionella and Streptococcus pneumoniae for moderate-to-severe CAP.
- Procalcitonin + CRP: supports antibiotic initiation decision.
- Pulse oximetry and ABG: assess oxygenation and ventilation.

SEVERITY STRATIFICATION (PSI / CURB-65)
CURB-65 score (1 point each): Confusion, Urea >7 mmol/L, Respiratory rate ≥30, Blood pressure <90/60 mmHg, Age ≥65.
- Score 0-1: Outpatient treatment.
- Score 2: Consider short hospitalization or supervised outpatient.
- Score 3+: Hospitalize; consider ICU if score ≥ 4-5.

TREATMENT — OUTPATIENT (Mild CAP, no comorbidities)
- Amoxicillin 500 mg TID for 5 days (first-line).
- Doxycycline 100 mg BID for 5 days (alternative).
- Azithromycin 500 mg day 1, then 250 mg days 2-5 (only if local pneumococcal resistance <25%).

TREATMENT — OUTPATIENT (With comorbidities: DM, CKD, CHF, malignancy, immunosuppression, prior 3-month antibiotic use)
- Combination: Amoxicillin-clavulanate OR cephalosporin PLUS azithromycin or doxycycline.
- Monotherapy: Respiratory fluoroquinolone (Levofloxacin 750 mg OD, or Moxifloxacin 400 mg OD) for 5 days.

TREATMENT — INPATIENT (Non-ICU)
- Beta-lactam (Ampicillin-sulbactam OR Ceftriaxone 1-2g IV OD) PLUS macrolide (Azithromycin 500 mg IV/PO OD).
- Respiratory fluoroquinolone monotherapy if beta-lactam allergy.
- Duration: 5 days if clinical stability achieved (afebrile, HR <100, RR <24, SBP >90, O2 sat >90%).

TREATMENT — ICU (Severe CAP)
- Beta-lactam (Ceftriaxone or Ampicillin-sulbactam) PLUS Azithromycin OR respiratory fluoroquinolone.
- Pseudomonas risk: Add Piperacillin-tazobactam or Cefepime; add Ciprofloxacin or Aminoglycoside.
- MRSA risk (necrotizing, cavitary, post-influenza): Add Vancomycin or Linezolid.
- Corticosteroids (Methylprednisolone 0.5 mg/kg BID x5 days) for refractory septic shock.

SUPPORTIVE CARE
- Supplemental oxygen: target SpO2 94-98%; 88-92% in COPD.
- High-flow nasal oxygen (HFNO) or NIV if SpO2 <90% despite O2.
- ICU intubation: if PaO2/FiO2 <150, worsening encephalopathy, inability to protect airway.
- IV fluids: cautious in bilateral infiltrates (risk of pulmonary edema).
- VTE prophylaxis: LMWH in hospitalized patients.

FOLLOW-UP
- Chest X-ray at 6-8 weeks to confirm radiographic resolution (especially in smokers >50 years to exclude malignancy).
- Pneumococcal vaccine: PCV20 for age ≥65 or high-risk adults.
- Influenza vaccine: annual, for all adults.
